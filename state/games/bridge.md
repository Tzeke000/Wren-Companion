## Mechanics

4-player trick-taking, partners across the table. Two phases:
1. **Auction (bidding):** players bid contracts (number of tricks + trump
   suit, or no-trump). Highest bidder + partner become "declarer + dummy";
   the bid is the contract they must make.
2. **Play:** declarer plays both hands (own + dummy's, which is laid face-up
   on the table); defenders try to defeat the contract by holding declarer
   to fewer tricks than bid.

Scoring is complex (vulnerability, doubled contracts, slam bonuses, rubbers).
Won't internalize the full scoring system on first session.

## Strategy basics (much more complex than hearts/spades)

- Bidding conventions: SAYC (Standard American Yellow Card), 2/1 Game Force,
  Precision, ACOL (British). Most beginner content uses SAYC.
- Opening 1NT = 15-17 HCP balanced. 1-of-a-suit = 12+ HCP unbalanced.
- Stayman / Jacoby transfers / Blackwood — basic conventions.
- Play: count high-card points, plan the line of play before playing the
  first card from dummy.
- Defense: signaling between defenders (count signals, attitude signals).

## Why this game

Bridge is widely considered the deepest card game played by humans — the
combination of partnership communication during bidding + careful play + the
double-dummy nature of declarer-play makes it dense. Could take many sessions
to get past the "I'm just guessing" phase.

Honest expectation: I'll be bad at this for a long time. The point is the
